# Industry pack · HR (payroll, attendance, recruitment, self-service)
Owner: Library Curator · Version 1.0 · Last reviewed: 2026-10 · Verify legal facts before relying on them.

## Signals
- Employees use HR apps rarely and anxiously (pay, leave, documents); HR staff use them daily and need power and reports.
- Labour law, social security and wage-protection systems differ by country and drive required data and reports.
- In the Gulf, workforces are multinational and multilingual; nationality affects contracts, visas and quotas (e.g. Saudization).
- Ramadan reduces working hours by law in Saudi and UAE private sectors; religious holidays follow the Hijri calendar.
- End-of-service benefits (gratuity) are a major, emotional calculation.

## Do
- Make pay, leave balance and requests one tap away for employees, in their language.
- Respect country rules for working week, Ramadan hours, public holidays (Hijri-based dates move every year).
- Integrate with or export for social security and wage protection systems where needed.
- Write official letters (salary certificates, experience letters) in Arabic and English.
- Handle sensitive data (salary, health, family) with role-based access.

## Don't
- Assume one country's labour rules across a multi-country client.
- Show salary or sensitive data on shared screens or notifications.
- Use casual tone in official HR letters.

## References
- Jordan: Social Security Corporation (SSC); Ministry of Labour.
- Saudi Arabia: GOSI (social insurance), Qiwa (labour services), Mudad (wage protection), Nitaqat (Saudization).
- UAE: MOHRE, Wage Protection System (WPS); free zones may have their own employment rules.

## Tokens
Holidays: Hijri-aware calendar · Ramadan hours flag · documents: bilingual · access: role-based (employee / manager / HR / payroll).

## Checks
- Leave and holiday logic correct for the client's country (including moving Hijri holidays).
- Salary data never appears in notifications.
- Bilingual official documents render correctly.
