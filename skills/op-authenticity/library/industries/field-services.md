# Industry pack · Field services (work orders, technicians, maintenance)
Owner: Library Curator · Version 1.0 · Last reviewed: 2026-10

## Signals
- Two worlds: the office (dispatchers, supervisors, desktop, dense) and the field (technicians, phone, outdoors, interrupted).
- Field conditions: strong sun and heat, gloves, dust, noise, rooftops and basements with poor signal.
- Multilingual crews are common in the Gulf (Arabic, English, Hindi/Urdu, Tagalog, Bengali); reading levels vary.
- Coordination already happens on WhatsApp and phone calls; photos and voice notes are the natural evidence.
- Addresses are imprecise; map pins, building names and landmarks are what technicians use.

## Do
- Offline-first job list with sync on reconnect; clear "saved on phone / sent" status.
- Big tap targets (≥ 48–56 px), high-contrast mode for sunlight, minimal typing.
- Photos, voice notes and signatures as proof of work; timestamp and location automatically.
- Icons plus short labels; support the crew's languages where reading is limited.
- Navigation via the map apps technicians already use (deep-link to Google Maps / Waze / local address systems).

## Don't
- Long forms in the field; tiny controls; light grey text on white.
- Require constant connectivity.
- Assume one language for the whole crew.

## References
- Address systems: Saudi National Address, Dubai Makani, map pins via WhatsApp in Jordan.
- Tools crews compare to: WhatsApp, Google Maps, Waze.

## Tokens
min tap target 56 px · contrast ≥ 7:1 for field screens · offline: required · media: photo, voice note, signature.

## Checks
- Complete a job fully offline, then sync.
- Usable with gloves and in bright light (test outdoors).
- A technician who reads little English can complete the top task.
