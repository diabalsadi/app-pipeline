# Discovery interview guide

Ask in roughly this order, one question at a time, and adapt the wording to the founder. Move on once an item is clearly answered, and skip anything the founder has already told you.

## Must know

1. **The idea in their words.** "Tell me about the app as you'd explain it to a friend."
2. **The problem.** "What problem does it solve? What happens today because this app doesn't exist?"
3. **Who has the problem.** "Who exactly has this problem? Describe one real person you have in mind."
4. **Current workaround.** "How do those people deal with it today?" This often reveals the true competitors, such as spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups, or paper.
5. **The main task.** "If someone used the app for 2 minutes, what is the one thing they would do?"
6. **Must-have features.** "What must the first version do? What could wait until later?" Push back gently on long lists.
7. **Money.** "How will the app make money, or is it free?" Examples: subscription, one-off purchase, commission on transactions, ads, or a free internal tool.
8. **Where it is used.** "Will people use it on their phone, on a computer, or both?"
9. **Competitors they know.** "Which apps or services do something similar? What do you like or dislike about them?"
10. **Success.** "Six months after launch, what would make you say it's working?" Turn the answer into one or two numbers.

## Nice to know

- **Brand feel.** "Pick three words for how the app should feel." This feeds the design stage.
- **Sensitive data.** Payments, health information, children, or location.
- **Budget.** "Is there a monthly budget for running costs?" This feeds the architecture stage.
- **Deadline.** "Is there a date this needs to be ready by, and why?"
- **Languages and countries** the app must support.
- **Hard requirements.** For example, a stack the founder must use or a system it must connect to. Record these exactly, because the architect stage only departs from its defaults for them.

## Follow-up habits

- When an answer is vague ("everyone", "lots of features"), ask for one concrete example.
- When the founder lists many features, ask which three they would keep if they could only keep three.
- When the founder asks you a technical question, answer it in plain words, then return to the interview.
