# Personas

The design stage uses two personas. Write both in `docs/03-design/personas.md`.

## User persona: who the app is for

A short, specific profile of the main user, built from the business plan and the interview notes. Specific details produce better design decisions than averages do.

```markdown
## User persona: [first name], [age]
- **Situation:** [job, life stage, where and when they would use the app]
- **Goal:** [what they want to get done]
- **Frustrations today:** [taken from the problem and current workaround]
- **Tech comfort:** [low, medium, or high, with one example]
- **Device and context:** [for example, "Android phone, one hand, on the bus"]
- **What would make them trust the app:** [one or two things]
```

Add a second user persona only if the requirements have a second role that uses many screens, such as an admin.

## Design persona: who is designing it

The designer you act as for this product. A design persona has a defined taste, so decisions come from a point of view rather than from defaults. It is written from the brand feel (three words), the user persona, and how the competitors look. The persona should differ visibly from the competitors.

```markdown
## Design persona
- **Who:** [a one-line designer identity, for example "an editorial designer who makes calm, print-inspired tools for people over 50"]
- **Influences:** [2 or 3 real-world references outside tech, for example Swiss rail signage, 1970s cookbooks, field guides]
- **Believes:** [3 design principles in their words, for example "Numbers are the hero; decoration is noise."]
- **Type:** [the kind of typefaces they reach for, and why]
- **Colour:** [their palette instinct, for example "ink and paper with one warm accent"]
- **Layout habits:** [for example, "left-aligned, generous margins, rules instead of boxes"]
- **Voice:** [how the copy sounds, with one example button and one example error message]
- **Would never:** [3 things this designer refuses to do]
```

### Examples

- A budgeting app for retirees: "A newspaper designer: serif headlines, tabular figures, hairline rules, one muted red for overspending."
- A swap app for Gen Z plant collectors: "A riso-print zine maker: two-colour overprints, chunky grotesk type, stickers as the signature element."
- A scheduling tool for clinics: "A wayfinding-signage designer: a high-contrast grid, a numbered system, colour used only to mean status."
